# Jim Simons — Twelve Decision Frameworks
Source: User-provided synthesis of Simons' public interviews and methodological principles.
Ingested: 2026-04-21

## Prompt 1: Data First, Models Second
*"We don't start with models. We start with data. We don't have any preconceived notions. We look for things that can be replicated thousands of times."*

Framework: (1) State your current theory. (2) Set it aside — what does raw data actually show? (3) Where is data incomplete or biased? (4) Is the pattern replicable thousands of times? (5) If data contradicts theory, follow the data.

## Prompt 2: The 51% Edge
Renaissance profits on barely more than 50% of trades. Not 80%. Not 90%. Just over 51%. Secret is volume — 300,000 trades/day at 51% win rate compounds massively. You don't need to be right most of the time. You need to be slightly right, repeatedly.

## Prompt 3: Hire the Outsiders
*"I never hired people from the investment world."* Recruited mathematicians, physicists, astronomers, speech recognition experts. Wall Street experience treated as a disadvantage — it embeds untested assumptions about how markets "should" work.

## Prompt 4: Never Override the Computer
Renaissance absolute rule: never override the model. Simons lost money every time he let emotion override his system (e.g., gold trade during Iraq-Kuwait war). After that, the rule became sacred. The system trades. Humans don't interfere.

## Prompt 5: Find the What, Not the Why
Renaissance looked for the 'what' of market moves, not the 'why.' If a pattern appeared reliably in the data, they traded it even without explanation. *"Any time you hear financial experts talking about how the market went up because of such and such, remember it's all nonsense."*

## Prompt 6: Layer the Signals
*"Many of the anomalies we initially exploited are intact, though they have weakened some. What you need to do is pile them up. You need to build a system that is layered and layered."* No single signal strong enough alone. Magic is in combining hundreds of weak signals into one powerful prediction.

## Prompt 7: Treat Failure as Data
Simons treated losses the same as winning trades — not as personal defeats, but as data. Wrote down every mistake. Tested why it happened. Built a rule to prevent it from happening again.

## Prompt 8: Reduce Position, Don't Panic
When Medallion's strategies hit volatility: system automatically reduced position sizes. Less exposure, same strategy. Worst decisions are made during chaos — don't change approach, shrink the bet until clarity returns.

## Prompt 9: The Monolithic Model
Most hedge funds run separate strategies independently. Simons did the opposite: single unified model where all strategies interconnected. Improvement in currency trading automatically improved stock trading. Everything talked to everything.

## Prompt 10: Extreme Secrecy and Competitive Moats
Employees sign most restrictive non-competes in industry. Code readable internally but nothing leaves the building. Biggest risk to any edge is other people copying it. Complexity is the moat — system so layered that even former employees couldn't recreate it alone.

## Prompt 11: Beauty as a Signal
*"Be guided by beauty. Just as a great theorem can be very beautiful, a company that's really working very well, very efficiently, can be beautiful."* Elegance as a filter. If a solution is inelegant or forced, it's probably wrong. If it's clean and beautiful, it's probably right.

## Prompt 12: The Full Simons Protocol
Master synthesis: Data First → 51% Edge → Outsider Thinking → Never Override → What Not Why → Layer the Signals → Failure as Data → Reduce Position → Monolithic Model → Competitive Moat → Beauty Test → The Move (single most strategic action based on all of the above).
