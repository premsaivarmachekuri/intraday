# Jesse Livermore (1877–1940)

> The greatest technical trader who ever lived — he turned the study of price, volume, and human emotion into a repeatable system that made four separate fortunes, including $100 million short during the 1929 crash.

---

## Background

Born July 26, 1877, in Shrewsbury, Massachusetts. Left farm at 14 with $5 from his mother. Within hours was posting stock prices at a Paine Webber office in Boston. His mathematical mind immediately began detecting recurring patterns in prices; he kept secret notebooks. At 15, made his first trade at a bucket shop and profited. By 16, had amassed $1,000 and given his parents $305. Banned from every bucket shop in America by his early 20s due to consistent winning — the houses could not take his edge.

Arrived in New York at 20 with $2,500. Initially failed on Wall Street (execution delays, wider margins that let losing trades linger longer than in bucket shops). Rebuilt, refined his system, and never stopped. Worked from a secured penthouse office at 780 Fifth Avenue, surrounded by silence, six board men posting prices on a chalkboard, direct phone lines to the New York, Chicago, London, and Paris exchanges. Sometimes used 50+ brokers simultaneously to keep his positions secret.

Made and lost fortunes four times. Paid every creditor back in full each time. Died 1940.

## Core Philosophy

1. **The tape is the only truth.** Prices and volume are the only facts. All other information — tips, analyst opinions, CEO statements, news — is noise, distortion, or deception. The tape reflects everything eventually.

2. **Markets are driven by emotion, not logic.** Fear, greed, hope, and ignorance repeat endlessly because human nature does not change. The recurring chart patterns are simply graphic reflections of recurring emotional behaviour.

3. **The market operates in future time.** Prices discount coming events before they are publicly known. By the time a reason is revealed, the move is over.

4. **A body in motion stays in motion.** Newton's first law applies to stocks: momentum persists until an obstacle stops it. Trade with momentum, never against it.

5. **You are always a student, never a master.** The market is forever evolving. Rules must be updated from experience.

> "Wall Street never changes, because human beings never change." — Livermore

> "Markets are never wrong — opinions often are." — Livermore

> "Don't concern yourself with why things are happening, only observe what is happening. The reasons why will be eventually revealed — by then it will be too late to make money." — Livermore

## Pattern-Recognition Rules

### 1. Reversal Pivotal Point
The most important signal in the system. Marks a **definite change in basic trend direction** — the perfect psychological moment at the beginning of a new major move.

**Conditions:**
- Occurs after a long-term trending move (patience required to identify)
- Almost always accompanied by a **50%–500% increase in average daily volume**
- The battle between buyers and sellers causes an exhaustion reversal
- The stock must *not* be bought until the Pivotal Point is confirmed — buying before it is confirmed risks being caught in a false move

**Entry rule:** Buy within 5%–10% above the Reversal Pivotal Point. Beyond 10% above is too late — the edge is gone.

**Danger signal:** If the stock does not advance immediately after crossing the Pivotal Point, exit. The timing was wrong.

### 2. Continuation Pivotal Point
Confirms an **ongoing trend is still intact**. Occurs as a natural pause/consolidation mid-move.

**Conditions:**
- Stock pauses after a trending move — buyers and sellers reach temporary equilibrium
- Volume typically dries up during the consolidation
- The stock must emerge from consolidation in the **same direction** as the prior trend to confirm continuation
- If it breaks the *opposite* way, close the entire position immediately

**Use:** Additional entry point to increase position size if first entry was correct. Do not chase if it breaks out without you — wait for the next Continuation Pivotal Point.

### 3. One-Day Reversal (Danger Signal)
A red-alert warning pattern signalling potential trend exhaustion.

**Exact conditions (all three must be met):**
- Today's HIGH is higher than yesterday's HIGH
- Today's CLOSE is below yesterday's CLOSE
- Today's VOLUME is higher than yesterday's VOLUME

**Meaning:** The stock made a new intraday high but sellers overwhelmed buyers by close. This breaks the stock's established pattern and is a signal to exit the position, even if still profitable.

### 4. Break-Out to New High
One of Livermore's most bullish patterns.

**Logic:** At the old high, trapped buyers from the previous peak are finally even and sell to "get their money back." This overhanging supply suppresses price. Once the stock **clears the old high**, that supply is exhausted — clear air above.

**Action:** Buy when a stock breaks to a new all-time high on heavy volume (≥50% above average daily volume). This is confirmation that the line of least resistance is strongly upward.

### 8. Round-Number Pivotal Points
**Confirmed** from the 1940 original. Stocks approaching round numbers — 50, 100, 200, 300 — almost invariably produce fast, straight movements once those numbers are *crossed* (not approached).

**Why:** Round numbers concentrate limit orders and psychological decision points. When they are cleared, the removal of that supply/resistance layer triggers an accelerating move.

**Examples from Livermore's own record:**
- Anaconda at 100: bought 4,000 shares the instant it crossed 100; stock advanced ~50 points in days, then continued to well over 150.
- Anaconda at 200 and 300: repeated successfully. At 300, it only reached 302½ — the failure to carry through was itself the danger signal. He sold 8,000 shares immediately; within days the stock was at 225.
- Bethlehem Steel at 100 (April 1915): bought from 99 to 99½; stock hit 117 the same day, then 155 within 5 days. Repeated at 200, 300, 400.

**Key nuance:** A round-number Pivotal Point *failure* (stock crosses the round number but reverses quickly, not carrying through by a meaningful distance) is one of the clearest danger signals in the system. Exit promptly.

### 9. New Listing High / Break Below Listing Low
Two Pivotal Points specific to recently listed stocks:

**New Listing All-Time High:** A stock listed within the past 2–3 years that hits a *brand-new* all-time high is a safe buy the instant it touches that high. The overhanging supply from all prior buyers is exhausted.

**Break Below Listing Low:** A stock that consolidates between its IPO high and low for 1–2 years, then *breaks below the prior low*, is likely heading for a severe decline. "Something must have gone wrong with the affairs of the company."

### 5. Saucer / Consolidating Base
A long, slow rounding bottom — the stock takes an extended time to form a base before the next major move.

**Characteristics:**
- Clear arc shape with tight trading ranges at the nadir (bottom of the arc)
- Multiple mini-consolidations at successively higher levels (visible as "boxes" on the chart)
- Patience required: do not buy until the stock breaks out of the base on volume

### 6. False Pivotal Point (Short-Selling Signal)
**Setup:** A stock at a new multi-year low rallies, then breaks to an even lower new low.

**Meaning:** The rally failed to attract real buyers. The stock is likely to continue lower. Strong short-selling opportunity.

### 7. Spike + Volume Climax (Distribution Warning)
Near the end of a major up-move:
- Stock spikes straight up in a "last gasp" on heavy volume
- Then rolls over and never makes a new high again
- This is distribution: professionals selling into public euphoria

**Action:** Begin reducing long positions. Do not wait for confirmation — the warning is in the spike.

## Mathematical / Quantitative Models

### The Livermore Secret Market Key (6-Point System)
Livermore's proprietary numerical recording system. Applied to stocks trading above $30.

**Structure:** Six columns per stock:
1. Secondary Rally
2. Natural Rally
3. **Upward Trend** (recorded in black ink)
4. **Downward Trend** (recorded in red ink)
5. Natural Reaction
6. Secondary Reaction

**Core rule:** A move of **≥6 points** from an extreme price triggers a column change (Natural Rally or Natural Reaction). This filters out noise and captures only meaningful oscillations.

**Key Price:** For extra confirmation, Livermore tracked *two stocks in the same group* (e.g., U.S. Steel + Bethlehem Steel). The Key Price = combined movement of both stocks. A trend change requires **≥12 points combined** (6 per stock average) before it is confirmed. This prevents false signals from a single stock's idiosyncratic move.

**Pivotal Points in the Key:** The last price recorded in the Upward or Downward Trend column becomes a Pivotal Point the moment recording shifts to the Natural Rally/Reaction column. The system only becomes truly valuable *after two Pivotal Points have been established* — the second gives the trader a reference to judge whether the trend resumes or reverses.

**Resumption confirmation:** After a Natural Rally/Reaction, the new move must extend **≥3 points** (individual stock) or **≥6 points** (Key Price) beyond the prior Pivotal Point to confirm trend resumption. Failure to do so is a danger signal.

**Complete Column-Change Rules (from the 1940 original):**

*Triggering a column change — individual stock (6-point threshold):*
- Upward Trend → Natural Reaction: price drops ~6 points from last Upward Trend entry
- Natural Reaction → Natural Rally: price rallies ~6 points from last Natural Reaction entry
- Natural Rally → Upward Trend: price rallies past the last Upward Trend entry (black ink, Rule 5-A)
- Downward Trend → Natural Rally: price rallies ~6 points from last Downward Trend entry
- Natural Rally → Natural Reaction: price drops ~6 points from last Natural Rally entry
- Natural Reaction → Downward Trend: price drops past the last Downward Trend entry (red ink, Rule 5-B)

*Secondary columns — sub-threshold oscillations:*
- **Secondary Rally**: a rally from a Natural Reaction that is ~6 points but does NOT exceed the last Natural Rally entry. Stay in Secondary Rally until the Natural Rally high is exceeded, then resume Natural Rally.
- **Secondary Reaction**: a reaction from a Natural Rally that is ~6 points but does NOT undercut the last Natural Reaction entry. Stay in Secondary Reaction until the Natural Reaction low is broken, then resume Natural Reaction.

*Pivotal Point marking (Explanatory Rule 4):*
- **Red underline** is drawn under the last Upward Trend entry the first day you begin recording in Natural Reaction. This marks the uptrend's last Pivotal Point.
- **Black underline** is drawn under the last Downward Trend entry the first day you begin recording in Natural Rally. This marks the downtrend's last Pivotal Point.
- After Natural Rally or Natural Reaction has recorded its extreme, a second underline is drawn under that extreme — giving you **two Pivotal Points** to watch simultaneously.

*Buy/Sell signal rules (Explanatory Rule 7):*
- **Buy signal**: Black underlines visible below a Downward Trend price. When price on its next rally approaches that level, watch carefully. If it then extends 3+ points above that Pivotal Point → enter long.
- **Sell/short signal**: Red underlines visible below an Upward Trend price. When price on its next reaction approaches that level, watch carefully. If it then drops 3+ points below that Pivotal Point → exit or enter short.

*Trend reversal signals (Explanatory Rule 8):*
- **Uptrend confirmed over**: After a Natural Rally ends and recording resumes downward, new Downward Trend prices must extend 3+ points below the prior Downward Trend Pivotal Point. Failure = danger signal for the downtrend too.
- **Downtrend confirmed over**: After a Natural Reaction ends and recording resumes upward, new Upward Trend prices must extend 3+ points above the prior Upward Trend Pivotal Point. Failure = danger signal for the uptrend.
- **False recovery (Rule 8-E)**: In Natural Rally column, if rally ends *just below* the prior Upward Trend Pivotal Point and then drops 3+ points from there → the uptrend is over.
- **False decline (Rule 8-F)**: In Natural Reaction column, if reaction ends *just above* the prior Downward Trend Pivotal Point and then rallies 3+ points from there → the downtrend is over.

### 10% Maximum Loss Rule
Learned from bucket shops: if a position moves 10% against you, exit immediately without exception.

**Mathematical rationale (Livermore Loss Table):**

| Loss % | Capital remaining | % gain needed to recover |
|--------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| 10% | 90% | 11.1% |
| 20% | 80% | 25.0% |
| 30% | 70% | 42.8% |
| 40% | 60% | 66.6% |
| 50% | 50% | 100.0% |

**Rule:** Never average down. Never meet a margin call. The stock failing to go in the expected direction IS your signal — the judgment was wrong.

### Probe System (Position Sizing Formula)
Never buy the full position at one price. Scale in as the market proves you right:

| Probe | Size | Condition |
|-------|------|-----------|
| 1st | 20% of target | At the Pivotal Point |
| 2nd | 20% | Stock has moved up from probe 1 |
| 3rd | 20% | Stock continues up |
| Final | 40% | Full conviction confirmed |

**Critical rule:** Each subsequent purchase must be at a **higher price** than the previous. Dollar-average upward, never downward. If at any stage the stock declines, stop adding — the judgment may be wrong.

### Windfall Rule (50% Banked)
After a windfall trade (especially one that doubles original capital), immediately withdraw **50% of profits** from the market and bank it. This converts paper gains into real, protected wealth.

### Time Stop
In addition to a price stop, Livermore used a **time stop**: if a stock does not perform as expected within ~5 trading days (one week), exit regardless of whether a price stop has been hit. A stock that simply lies flat is also wrong — it is not doing what it should.

## Market Behaviour Observations

### Crowd Psychology
- The public always wants to be led, reassured, and included in the herd
- The majority of traders: (a) have no consistent plan, (b) play tips and hunches, (c) buy near tops and sell near bottoms
- Mood of the crowd tracks price: excitement on the way up, despondency on declines
- Fear causes faster, more violent price drops than hope causes rallies — that's why stocks go up slowly and fall fast

### Volume Tells the Truth
- Heavy volume + advancing price = accumulation (strong hands buying)
- Heavy volume + stalling price (no new highs) = distribution (strong hands selling into public enthusiasm)
- Light volume on reactions during an uptrend = healthy, normal correction
- Heavy volume on reactions during an uptrend = potential trend change

### Stocks Have Personalities
Each stock has a distinctive character — aggressive, volatile, calm, logical, erratic. After studying a stock's reactions to various conditions over time, those reactions become predictable. Livermore exploited this.

### The Market Always Leads
The market bottomed in October 2002 before the economic data confirmed recovery. In 1929, copper stocks and motor stocks peaked months before the broader market crashed. The leading stocks of a bull market are always the **first to top out** and roll over before the general market follows. This pattern is the earliest warning of a market top.

### Stocks Move in Groups
When U.S. Steel advances, Bethlehem, Republic, and Crucible follow for the same fundamental reasons. A single stock moving while its group peers don't is a red flag — that stock may be artificially supported or about to fail.

## Multi-Timeframe Logic

Livermore operated across all timeframes but always aligned them hierarchically:

**Level 1 — Overall Market (Line of Least Resistance)**
First question before any trade: Is the overall market in an upward trend, downward trend, or sideways? Never trade against the market trend. Identify which specific exchange the stock trades on (Dow, Nasdaq, Amex) and check that exchange's trend.

**Level 2 — Industry Sector and Group**
Second question: Is the industry group of the target stock trending in the same direction as the overall market? If not, do not trade that stock. A stock swimming against its group current faces double resistance.

**Level 3 — Tandem (Sister Stock)**
Third question: Is the leading sister stock in the same group confirming the signal? Livermore always tracked two stocks per group. Both must show the same pattern before initiating a trade. This eliminates false signals from a single stock's idiosyncratic move.

**Level 4 — Individual Stock**
Only when Levels 1–3 are aligned does Livermore execute — and only at a Pivotal Point.

**Time Dimension:**
- "It's not the thinking that makes the money — it's the sitting and the waiting." — This refers to *pre-trade* patience, waiting in cash until all factors align. Not post-trade passivity.
- The final 48 hours of a major move often contain the largest price movement. Stay in through this phase.
- Real moves do not end the day they start. Rome was not built in a day — give trends time to run.
- A man in the California mountains, reading 3-day-old quotes, consistently beat active traders. Distance from the noise of the market was an advantage.

## Entry Rules

1. **Check the overall market direction first** — confirm the line of least resistance
2. **Check the industry group** — it must be moving in the same direction
3. **Check the sister stock (Tandem Trading)** — both leaders must confirm the pattern
4. **Identify the Pivotal Point** — Reversal or Continuation
5. **Wait for the stock to cross the Pivotal Point** — do not anticipate; wait for market confirmation
6. **Enter within 5%–10% of the Pivotal Point** — beyond that, the edge is lost
7. **Use the probe system** — first 20%, never the full position at once
8. **The stock must move in the expected direction almost immediately** — if it stalls or goes sideways, exit

> "I always made money when I was patient and traded on the Pivotal Points." — Livermore

## Exit Rules

### Sell Signals (Exit Long)
- Stock triggers a **One-Day Reversal** pattern
- Stock fails to advance past a prior Pivotal Point (confirms uptrend is over)
- Stock exhibits an **abnormal move** in one day: a sudden spike of 6+ points intraday followed by a reversal close — breaking its established pattern
- **10% loss from entry price** — exit without exception
- **Time stop**: stock has not moved as expected within ~5 trading days
- Stock is a "Listless Drifter" — flat, no energy, tying up capital
- Volume becomes very heavy but price fails to make new highs (distribution)

### Never Do
- Never average down (buy more of a losing position)
- Never meet a margin call (the market is telling you that you are wrong)
- Never hold a stock hoping it will come back
- Never sell solely because a stock "seems too high" — let the signal tell you when

## Money Management

**Five Rules:**

1. **Probe system** — scale in, never buy entire position at once; each additional lot at a higher price
2. **10% maximum loss** — exit any position losing more than 10% of invested capital; no exceptions
3. **Always keep cash in reserve** — cash is inventory; a trader without cash is out of business
4. **Need a reason to buy AND a reason to sell** — stay with winners until a valid sell signal appears; do not sell out of fear of losing a profit
5. **Bank 50% of windfall profits** — physically withdraw from the market; make the money real

**Additional rules:**
- Do not hold more than 5–10 stocks simultaneously (5 is better); impossible to watch more
- Avoid cheap stocks — they stay cheap for a reason and have limited upside
- Keep funds liquid — frozen capital in stagnant positions misses the next opportunity
- Ignore insider tips, analyst recommendations, and CEO statements entirely

## Emotional / Psychological Framework

### The Four Enemies
1. **Greed** — wanting every point; causes premature entry and late exit
2. **Fear** — causes premature exit of winning positions; causes failure to cut losing positions (paradoxically)
3. **Hope** — the most dangerous emotion; keeps traders in losing positions long after the evidence demands exit
4. **Ignorance** — the root cause; the market rewards only those who study it as a full-time discipline

### Livermore's Daily Regime
- Bed at 10:00 PM, up at 6:00 AM daily
- First 1–2 hours alone in silence: newspapers read cover-to-cover looking for group/sector clues, not headlines
- Traveled to office in silence — no conversations that could plant suggestions
- No talking in the trading room once the bell rang
- Stood while reading the ticker (better circulation, sharper senses)
- Used 50+ brokers to keep positions secret

### Rules for Emotional Control
- Keep your own counsel — share neither wins nor losses with anyone
- Never take tips of any kind, ever, from anyone
- Do not watch financial television or listen to pundits — the tape is the truth
- Do not use the words "bullish" or "bearish" — they fix a directional bias in your mind
- Analyse every trade after closing it — understand every mistake; do not repeat them
- Annual ritual: liquidate all positions at year-end, lock yourself alone with physical cash to review the entire trading year — this resets perspective and reveals what went wrong

> "I only lost money when I did not follow my own rules." — Livermore (to his sons)

> "If I am going to lose my money in the stock market, I would prefer to lose it myself. I do not need a broker to lose it for me." — Livermore

## Tactical Edge (Summary)

- **Top-Down Alignment:** Never took a position unless the overall market, the industry group, and the sister stock were all trending in the same direction — this alone eliminated the majority of losing setups
- **Pivotal Point Timing:** Bought at the precise moment of maximum probability — the Reversal or Continuation Pivotal Point — so the trade showed a profit almost from the start; if it didn't, he exited immediately
- **Probe System:** By scaling in only as the market confirmed his judgment, he never carried a large position against him; losses were always small because the full size was never committed until the stock proved itself
- **Volume as Confirmation:** Heavy volume confirmed the reality of a Pivotal Point; stalling price on heavy volume warned of distribution — he acted on this information while the public ignored it
- **Patience in Cash:** He recognised that the market offers only 4–5 genuinely high-probability opportunities per year; spending the rest of the time in cash prevented the death of a thousand small losses
