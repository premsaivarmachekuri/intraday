# Entry–Exit Framework

> Universal rules for when to initiate, scale into, and close any position — distilled from Livermore's 45-year system.

---

## What It Does

Provides a complete decision tree for trade management from first entry through final exit. Removes the emotional element from individual trade decisions by pre-defining all conditions in advance.

---

## Pre-Trade Checklist (run before every trade)

- [ ] Overall market trend confirmed (line of least resistance)?
- [ ] Sector trend aligned?
- [ ] Industry group trend aligned?
- [ ] Sister/tandem stock showing same pattern?
- [ ] Individual stock at Pivotal Point (Reversal or Continuation)?
- [ ] Volume confirming Pivotal Point (≥50% above average)?
- [ ] Entry within 5–10% of Pivotal Point?
- [ ] Profit target established (minimum 10 points potential move)?
- [ ] Stop price set (10% from entry)?
- [ ] Time stop set (~5 trading days)?
- [ ] Probe 1 size calculated (20% of target position)?
- [ ] Cash reserve maintained after this trade?

**If any of the first 7 items is "No" → Do not trade.**

---

## Entry Logic

**Entry 1 (Probe):**
- At or within 5–10% above a Reversal Pivotal Point, or at the breakout from a Continuation Pivotal Point
- Size: 20% of target position
- This is a test, not a commitment

**Entry 2:**
- Only if Entry 1 is showing a profit
- Must be at a higher price than Entry 1
- Size: 20% of target position

**Entry 3:**
- Only if Entries 1 and 2 are both showing a profit
- Must be at a higher price than Entry 2
- Size: 20% of target position

**Entry 4 (Full size):**
- Only if all prior entries profitable
- Can also occur at a valid Continuation Pivotal Point breakout during the move
- Can also occur at a breakout to a new all-time high on heavy volume
- Size: 40% of target position

**Never:**
- Buy before the Pivotal Point forms (anticipation = speculation without confirmation)
- Buy more than 10% above a Reversal Pivotal Point (the edge is gone)
- Buy a declining stock ("don't buy on reactions")
- Short a rallying stock ("don't short on rallies")

---

## Hold Logic

**Hold the position as long as:**
- The stock is making new highs (longs) or new lows (shorts)
- No abnormal pattern has appeared (One-Day Reversal, volume climax)
- The overall market trend and group trend remain aligned
- No valid sell signal has appeared
- The stock is acting "right" — normal reactions, then resumption of trend

> "As long as the action of the market does not give you any cause to worry, have the courage of your convictions and stay with it." — Livermore

**Patience rules:**
- The final 48 hours of a major move often contain the largest price gains — stay in
- Real moves do not end the day they start — give the trend time to run
- If the stock is acting right and the market is right, do not be in a hurry to take a profit

## Exit Logic

### Hard Exits (immediate, no discretion)
| Trigger | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Price drops 10% from entry | Exit entire position |
| Margin call received | Exit immediately — market says you are wrong |
| Time stop: 5 trading days, stock flat or wrong direction | Exit |
| One-Day Reversal pattern appears | Exit or reduce significantly |
| Stock reverses 6+ points intraday and closes near low | Danger signal — exit |

### Soft Exits (judgment required — look for confirmation)
| Signal | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Heavy volume + price fails to make new high | Begin reducing position |
| Blow-off spike up on massive volume, then rollover | Exit all longs |
| Overall market trend reverses | Re-evaluate all positions |
| Industry group leaders start rolling over | Reduce position |
| Sister/tandem stock breaks down while stock holds | Warning — start reducing |
| Stock becomes "Listless Drifter" — flat, no energy | Exit — capital is frozen |

### Profit Exits
- **Do not** sell solely because a stock "seems too high" — this is a common, expensive mistake
- **Do** sell when a valid sell signal appears (One-Day Reversal, volume climax, failed Pivotal Point)
- After a windfall trade (doubles capital), bank 50% of profits immediately

---

## Psychological Guardrails

1. Write down the stop price and time stop **before** entering the trade — bind yourself to them
2. If the stock moves against you by any amount immediately after entry, consider exiting — the timing may simply be wrong
3. Do not give back profits out of fear — "you know you are right because if you were not, you would have no profit at all"
4. Do not hold a loser out of hope — every day held in a losing position is capital that cannot be deployed in the next winner
5. Paper losses are real losses — they do not become real only when sold
