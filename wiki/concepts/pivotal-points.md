# Pivotal Points

> The precise moment in price action where a new major move begins or an existing move confirms continuation — the only timing signal Livermore trusted.

---

## Description

A Pivotal Point is a price level where the balance of power between buyers and sellers shifts decisively, producing either a change in trend (Reversal Pivotal Point) or a resumption of trend after a pause (Continuation Pivotal Point). Livermore was the first trader to formally name and systematise this concept.

Pivotal Points are not arbitrary support/resistance levels. They form from actual market action — the exhaustion of one side, confirmed by a surge in volume. They are the market's own declaration of its next direction.

> "When a speculator can determine the Pivotal Point of a stock and interpret the action at that point, he may make a commitment with the positive assurance of being right from the start." — Livermore

---

## Types

### Reversal Pivotal Point
Marks a **definite change in basic trend direction**.

**Characteristics:**
- Forms after a long-term trending move (either up or down)
- Accompanied by a climactic surge in volume (50%–500% above average daily volume)
- Buyers and sellers battle intensely — one side exhausts the other
- The stock reverses direction from this level

**Action:** Enter a position in the *new* direction. The entry must be within 5%–10% of the Pivotal Point. Beyond 10% is too late — the edge is gone.

**Failure signal:** If the stock does not move in the expected direction immediately after the Pivotal Point, exit. The judgment was wrong.

### Continuation Pivotal Point
Confirms that **an existing trend is still intact** after a normal pause.

**Characteristics:**
- Stock pauses mid-trend (buyers and sellers temporarily equal)
- Volume dries up during the consolidation
- Stock must emerge from consolidation **in the same direction** as the prior trend
- If it breaks the opposite way, close the entire position

**Action:** Additional entry point to increase position size after the stock breaks out of the consolidation in the trend direction.

### False Pivotal Point
A rally from a new low that **fails** and breaks to an even lower new low.

**Meaning:** No genuine buying support at the new low. The stock is likely to continue lower. Classic short-selling setup.

### Round-Number Pivotal Point
Specific price levels — 50, 100, 200, 300 — that act as high-probability Pivotal Points. Livermore documented repeated instances of explosive moves whenever these levels were *crossed* (Anaconda, Bethlehem Steel). The round number concentrates supply from limit sellers and psychological exits; clearing it removes all that resistance at once.

**Failure is the danger signal:** If the stock crosses the round number but fails to carry through by a meaningful distance, that *failure* is one of the clearest sell/short signals in the system.

### New-High Pivotal Point
When a relatively recently listed stock (within 2–3 years) hits a **brand-new all-time high**, the removal of trapped buyers means the line of least resistance is decisively upward. Buy the instant it touches a new all-time high.

### Break-Below-Prior-Low Pivotal Point
A stock that consolidates between its listing high and low for 1–2 years, then *breaks below the prior low*, signals fundamental deterioration. A severe drop is likely coming.

## Pioneer Perspectives

**Jesse Livermore** — originator. Applied Pivotal Points as the sole timing mechanism. Never bought before one formed; never held a position that failed to perform after one. The Secret Market Key is a mechanical system for tracking Pivotal Points numerically across two stocks simultaneously. See [Jesse Livermore](../pioneers/jesse-livermore.md).

---

## Key Rules

1. Never enter before the Pivotal Point forms — anticipation is the enemy
2. Buy within 5%–10% of the Reversal Pivotal Point; beyond that, skip the trade
3. The stock must advance almost immediately after the Pivotal Point — if it stalls, exit
4. A Continuation Pivotal Point that breaks the wrong way is an immediate exit signal
5. Volume confirmation is essential — a Pivotal Point on low volume is suspect
6. Track two stocks per group (Key Price) for maximum confirmation reliability
7. **3-point rule (individual stock):** After a Natural Reaction/Rally ends, the resuming trend must extend 3+ points *beyond* the prior Pivotal Point to confirm resumption; failure is a danger signal
8. **6-point rule (Key Price):** Same logic applied to the two-stock combined price — requires 6-point extension beyond the Key Price Pivotal Point
9. **Round numbers:** Treat 50, 100, 200, 300 as automatic Pivotal Points; a failure to carry through after crossing them is an immediate exit signal
