# Trend Following — Line of Least Resistance

> Trading in the direction of the dominant price momentum until a confirmed reversal, never fighting the prevailing force.

---

## Description

Livermore called it the **Line of Least Resistance** — the direction in which price is moving with the least opposition. He deliberately avoided the terms "bull market" and "bear market" because those words create a fixed mental bias. "Upward trend" and "downward trend" are dynamic descriptions; they can change. Labels like "bull" cannot be easily overridden by the mind.

The Line of Least Resistance is the first thing to determine before any trade. Everything else — sector, group, individual stock — must align with it.

> "He did not care which direction the market was moving. There was always a trade possible for him, either up or down, short or long. He always wanted the wind at his back." — Smitten

---

## Pioneer Perspectives

**Jesse Livermore:**
- Determined the Line of Least Resistance before any trade by checking the overall market trend of the specific exchange the target stock traded on (Dow, Nasdaq, or Amex — not a generic "market")
- Applied the principle hierarchically: market → sector → industry group → individual stock
- Equally comfortable on the short side — the market goes up ~1/3 of the time, sideways ~1/3, and down ~1/3; a trader who only goes long is out of the game 2/3 of the time
- Momentum is like a tidal wave: "once set in motion it will remain in motion until it hits an obstacle and stops or is reversed"

---

## Synthesis

Trend-following is not prediction — it is *confirmation*. The trader waits for the market to declare its direction, then aligns with it. This is the opposite of forecasting. The discipline is in resisting the urge to anticipate.

The key insight from Livermore: the same momentum principle that governs individual stocks governs industry groups and the overall market. A group that is leading the market higher will be the **first to roll over** when the market tops. Watching group leadership is an early warning system for overall market reversals.

---

## Key Rules

1. Identify the line of least resistance for the market, sector, group, and stock before trading
2. Never trade against the trend of any level — market, group, or stock
3. Use the words "upward trend" and "downward trend" — never "bull" or "bear" (they bias the mind)
4. The leading stocks and groups of one bull market are rarely the leaders of the next
5. When leading stocks/groups start rolling over, the broader market top is near
6. Do not go short on a rally; do not go long on a reaction — enter in the direction of trend
7. Trade both sides (long and short) — the market goes up, sideways, and down roughly equally
