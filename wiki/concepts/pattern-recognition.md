# Pattern Recognition

> Chart patterns are not technical abstractions — they are the graphic fingerprints of recurring human emotional states that have repeated across every market era.

---

## Description

Livermore's foundational insight was that markets are driven by human emotion (fear, greed, hope, ignorance) — and human nature does not change. Therefore, the price patterns produced by these emotions repeat. Stocks at different times and in different eras trace the same formations because the same emotional sequences are playing out. Pattern recognition is really *emotional recognition*.

> "The formations and patterns recur on a constant basis. The patterns the trader observes are simply the reflections of human emotional behaviour." — Livermore

---

## Key Patterns (Livermore Taxonomy)

### 1. Reversal Pivotal Point
**Emotional state:** Exhaustion — one side (buyers or sellers) has pushed price to an extreme and runs out of firepower. The opposing force overwhelms them on heavy volume and reverses direction.
**What it looks like:** A dramatic price turn on climactic volume after a sustained trend.
**Trade:** Enter in the *new* direction within 5–10% of the reversal point.

### 2. Continuation Pivotal Point / Base
**Emotional state:** Consolidation — neither bulls nor bears dominate; the stock rests after a move, absorbing supply or demand before resuming.
**What it looks like:** A sideways tight-range pause mid-trend, volume dries up, then price breaks out in the prior trend direction.
**Trade:** Add to position on the breakout from the base.

### 3. One-Day Reversal
**Emotional state:** Intraday exhaustion — buyers push price to a new high intraday but sellers overwhelm them by close. The stock closes below the prior close on higher volume.
**What it looks like:** Today's high > yesterday's high; today's close < yesterday's close; today's volume > yesterday's volume.
**Trade:** Exit or reduce longs immediately. This is a danger signal.

### 4. New High Breakout
**Emotional state:** Supply exhaustion — all trapped buyers from the prior peak have finally sold "even" or given up. Price clears old resistance on heavy volume.
**What it looks like:** Price closes above a significant prior high on volume ≥50% above average.
**Trade:** Buy — clear air above the breakout level.

### 5. Saucer / Rounding Bottom
**Emotional state:** Gradual transition — bears exhausted over an extended period; no dramatic capitulation, just slow accumulation. Price traces a curved, extended bottom.
**What it looks like:** Long arc shape, tight trading ranges at the nadir, multiple mini-consolidations at successively higher levels.
**Trade:** Buy on the breakout from the saucer rim.

### 6. Volume Spike / Blow-Off Top
**Emotional state:** Euphoric distribution — public buying at maximum enthusiasm as professionals exit.
**What it looks like:** Sudden vertical price spike on massive volume, then the stock rolls over and fails to make a new high.
**Trade:** Exit all longs immediately. The move is over.

### 7. False Pivotal Point
**Emotional state:** Failed recovery — a bounce from a new low attracts no real buyers; the stock rolls back and makes an even lower new low.
**What it looks like:** Rally from a new low that fails to sustain; stock drops through the prior new low.
**Trade:** Short — no genuine support; likely to continue lower.

## Pioneer Perspectives

**Jesse Livermore:** First to formally name and systematise Pivotal Points as the primary pattern. Used a 6-column numerical recording system (the Secret Market Key) rather than charts, but the patterns he tracked are identically visible on charts. His insight: the *same patterns* recur because the *same emotions* recur — across time, across markets, across eras. See [Jesse Livermore](../pioneers/jesse-livermore.md).

---

## Key Rules

1. A pattern is only as strong as its volume confirmation
2. Never anticipate a pattern's direction — wait for the breakout
3. A pattern failure is a signal in itself (False Pivotal Point = short opportunity)
4. The strongest patterns occur after long trending moves — patience to wait for them
5. Personalities of stocks: each stock's patterns are predictable once you know its character
